Research Question
Does Colony Collapse Disorder generate measurable causal effects on farm-gate crop prices, and does the magnitude of those effects vary systematically with a crop's biological dependence on managed honeybee pollination?

Summary of the conversation
The discussion began with a sketch of a research idea examining CCD's downstream price effects. The initial framing established that the data constraints largely dictate study design: the most severely affected crops — almonds, blueberries, tart cherries — have no liquid futures markets, ruling out a derivatives-based approach and confining the study to USDA farm-gate spot prices. A credible design was outlined combining USDA prices received by state, state×year colony loss rates as treatment intensity, and an event-study window around the October 2006 USDA CCD alert.
The second exchange addressed prior literature. The canonical reference is Rucker, Thurman & Burgett (2019, JAERE), which studies pollination fees and colony input markets — not downstream crop prices. That distinction is the gap this paper fills. The 2008 financial crisis is the main confounder, manageable via a triple-difference design contrasting high-dependency versus low-dependency crops within the same states and seasons. The appropriate outlet is a full-length paper targeting JAERE as the first choice, AJAE second, JEEM third if the biodiversity framing is foregrounded.
The third exchange mapped all data sources in detail: the NASS Honey Bee Colonies report (quarterly, state-level, 2015–present) for the formal colony loss series; the Bee Informed Partnership / Apiary Inspectors of America survey (2007–2023) hosted at usbeedata.org to fill the critical pre-2015 event window; NASS Honey report annual colony counts back to 1986 for an implied loss proxy enabling long pre-period testing; and NASS QuickStats for crop prices across treatment and control commodities. Bee-dependency ratios by crop come from Ferrier et al. (2018, USDA ERS EIB-246).
